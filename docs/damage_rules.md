# Concealed-damage rules

The contract requires "concealed-damage flags **with the rule that fired**". Every flag in
`result.json` carries a `rule_id` from this file, plus the evidence that triggered it.

These are explicit rules, not a learned classifier, and that is deliberate. A classifier can
say "0.8 mould"; it cannot say *which rule* it applied, because it does not apply rules. A
surveyor acting on a concealed-damage flag needs to know it fired because damage sits within
300 mm of the floor — a claim they can inspect and disagree with — not because a network was
confident. Rules are auditable and contestable, which is what makes the claim defensible.

Concealed damage is by definition not visible. Every rule below is therefore an inference
from visible evidence to likely hidden extent, and each carries a confidence well under 1.0.

| Rule ID | Fires when | Why hidden damage is likely | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R1-RISING-DAMP` | damage within **300 mm** of the floor | moisture tracking up from the slab or a failed damp-proof course usually extends further inside the wall than the visible face suggests | 0.55 |
| `R2-CEILING-LEAK` | damage within **300 mm** of the ceiling | water entering from above spreads along the ceiling void before it shows on the wall, so the wetted area is typically larger than what is seen | 0.55 |
| `R3-EXTENSIVE` | a single region larger than **0.50 m²** | damage of this extent rarely stops at the surface; substrate behind it should be assumed affected until opened up | 0.50 |
| `R4-DEEP-DISTORTION` | surface departs from the wall plane by more than **30 mm** | plaster displaced this far has usually lost adhesion over a wider area than the part that has visibly moved | 0.60 |
| `R5-SHARED-SURFACE` | **two or more** separate regions on one wall | multiple defects on a single surface point to a common cause behind it rather than to independent local damage | 0.45 |
| `R6-OPENING-ADJACENT` | damage within **500 mm** of a door or window | reveals and lintels are the usual path for water and for movement, and damage at an opening is generally worse inside the reveal than on the face | 0.40 |

## Scope items

Each flag and each damage region generates repair items keyed to the surface they sit on, so
every line in `scope[]` names a `surface_id` and a `because` pointing at either a damage
region or a rule. Quantities carry intervals; items generated by a *rule* rather than by a
measured region are provisional by nature and carry a 30% sigma to say so.

## Limits of the detector these rules run on

Regions are found geometrically, as departures from the fitted wall plane. That yields a
metric extent directly, which is what the contract asks for, and it is measured rather than
estimated.

What it cannot do is **name** a defect. Peeling paint, a water stain and mould are matters of
appearance, and a stain that has not lifted the plaster is perfectly flat — geometrically
invisible. So `class` is inferred from shape alone, every region carries
`class_source: "shape"`, and confidence never exceeds 0.5. A vision-language model over the
RGB frames is the right instrument for naming and is the intended next step.

Measured behaviour (`tests/test_damage.py`, and `bench/gates.py` over the supplied captures):

- a 40 × 30 cm patch lifted 25 mm is detected, extent correct to within 12 cm
- a wardrobe standing 40 cm off the wall is **not** reported: blistered plaster lifts by
  millimetres, and depth is what separates a defect from furniture
- 4 mm of noise is not reported
- three real, undamaged flats produce **zero** regions

The false-positive rate is therefore measured and is zero on the available data. The
**true-positive rate on real damage is unverified**, because no capture of a genuinely damaged
room exists in this benchmark — the one damaged room available was photographed on a
non-LiDAR device. That gap is stated rather than papered over.
